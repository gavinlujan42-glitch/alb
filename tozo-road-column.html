TOZO'S ROAD NOTES // Transmission 001 // Somewhere north of Socorro, probably
TOZO

No permanent address. No press badge. A notebook with coffee on it, three good pencils, a questionable road map, and enough curiosity to pull over whenever New Mexico starts glowing funny.

Who the hell is TOZO?

TOZO is ALB's wandering correspondent, a modern high-desert hobo in the old American sense of the word: itinerant, observant, allergic to schedules, moving by bus, boot, borrowed pickup, cheap motel, campground and whatever road looks interesting at sunset. He might surface in Taos listening to a ceramicist explain the universe, disappear into the Gila for a week, then file copy from a laundromat in Gallup because the Wi-Fi at the motel died.

He is a fictional ALB character and unreliable narrator, which means the feelings may be true even when the mileage is approximate. He reports on people, landscape, roadside America, art, food, politics, weather, weirdness and the little republics of conversation that appear wherever two strangers share coffee.

Entry One: The Road Does Not Have A Press Secretary

I woke up with the sun drilling through a motel curtain the color of old mustard and absolutely no idea whether I was supposed to be in Española, Taos, or some metaphysical jurisdiction between the two.

The truck stop coffee was strong enough to negotiate water rights. Outside, a raven inspected the parking lot with the administrative confidence of a cabinet secretary. Somewhere up the road, somebody was selling piñon, somebody else was selling turquoise, and at least four people were explaining New Mexico to a tourist who had made the rookie mistake of asking.

This is the territory, carnal. Adobe and fiber optic. Lowrider chrome flashing beside a solar array. Santos, satellites, chile smoke, yoga mats, acequias, laboratories, pueblos, old hippies, young coders, ranchers, artists, engineers and one dude at the co-op who may or may not have personally discovered consciousness in 1973.

There is a particular frequency around Taos where the radio signal gets thin and every mesa starts looking like it knows something. Maybe it's altitude. Maybe it's geology. Maybe somebody put too much mushroom powder in the cacao. I am not qualified to determine causation.

What I know is that New Mexico refuses to become one thing. That's the whole racket. Santa Fe can sell a five-thousand-dollar painting three blocks from somebody fixing a 1994 Chevy in the dirt. Los Alamos can calculate the edge of physics while an acequia association downstream argues about a gate somebody's grandfather installed. Albuquerque can be aerospace before lunch and carne adovada by dinner.

So I keep moving. Not because I am lost. Because the road keeps changing the question.

My assignment, insofar as ALB has assignments, is to find the signal underneath the official signal. The conversation after the meeting. The diner booth. The rez dog asleep in the shade. The artist building something nobody asked for. The bureaucrat quietly holding an entire program together with an Excel sheet and stubbornness. The kid staring west and wondering whether to leave. The old timer staring west and wondering why anybody ever did.

Tomorrow I might go north. I might go west. There is a rumor about a hot spring, a UFO mural and a green-chile cheeseburger that has allegedly altered several people's political affiliations.

That's enough journalism for one day.

Nos vemos somewhere down the road.

TOZO
