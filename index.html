A city with its tie loosened

Albuquerque is not a backdrop. It is a frequency. It hums through neon motel signs, acequia gates, zoning hearings, food-truck generators, server racks, courtroom fluorescents, and the electrical weather gathering over the Sandias.

ALB is a satirical magazine and midnight civic broadcast about power, water, technology, politics, culture, survival, beauty, and the elaborate paperwork people use to avoid saying what they mean.

Editorial oathWe punch up. We verify facts before bending the mirror. We distinguish satire from reporting. We respect New Mexico’s people, histories, lands, and living cultures. We question power without dehumanizing the powerless.

Enter Jack Nightshade

“Ladies and gentlemen, drifters, coders, ditch riders, night clerks, failed prophets, and city employees still answering email after dark: this is Jack Nightshade, your local jackalope, coming to you live from an airship parked somewhere above Taos.”

Jack is the next-generation desert cat: part hippie, part hipster, part cyber-noir disc jockey, part reluctant civic auditor. He came to Taos for two weeks, stayed for two years, and now claims the airship has rent control.

His program follows the money, tests the mythology, reads the footnotes, and keeps one eye on the stars. No shouting contest. No profanity crutch. No worship of authority. Just the signal, the evidence, the joke, and the uncomfortable pause afterward.
